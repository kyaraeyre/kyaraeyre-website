Paradise Valley, Arizona real estate

Tucked between Camelback Mountain and Mummy Mountain, Paradise Valley is defined by large-lot privacy and some of the lowest housing density in the Valley, with homes generally ranging from $2 million to $30 million or more.

What is Paradise Valley known for?

Paradise Valley is known for large-lot privacy, mountain views, and some of the lowest housing density in the metro Phoenix area. It is its own incorporated town, not a neighborhood of Phoenix or Scottsdale, and it sits between the two, bordered by Camelback Mountain to the south and centered near Mummy Mountain.

The town deliberately limits commercial development, which keeps most retail and dining concentrated at its edges, near resort properties like Sanctuary on Camelback Mountain and the Sanctuary/El Chorro corridor. That low commercial density is part of what defines the town's character and, in turn, its property values.

What do homes cost in Paradise Valley?

Home prices in Paradise Valley generally run from about $2 million to $30 million or more. Where a property falls in that range depends heavily on lot size, unobstructed mountain views, subdivision, and whether the home is a renovated original or new construction. Estates with direct Camelback Mountain views or large usable lots in subdivisions like Echo Canyon and Camelback Country Club Estates tend to command the upper end of the range. Buyers should confirm current comparable sales with a licensed agent before setting price expectations for a specific subdivision, since luxury pricing in Paradise Valley can vary block by block.

What are Paradise Valley's notable subdivisions and gated communities?

Several subdivisions define the Paradise Valley market. Clearwater Hills and Palo Cristi are established, well-known enclaves. Paradise Valley Country Club Estates and Camelback Country Club Estates both include guard-gated or privately gated sections built around country club access. Echo Canyon, at the base of Camelback Mountain, is known for dramatic lots and direct mountain proximity.

Gating isn't universal in Paradise Valley, though. Much of the town consists of open, non-gated streets. Beyond the named subdivisions above, it's not unusual for individual estates elsewhere in town to add their own perimeter walls and private gated drives, but plenty of Paradise Valley properties rely on large lots and low density for privacy rather than a guard gate.

What are the lifestyle anchors in Paradise Valley?

Camelback Mountain and Mummy Mountain are the two defining landmarks, and both offer public hiking access alongside the privacy of the estates around them. The Sanctuary/El Chorro area anchors much of the town's dining and resort activity, including Elements at Sanctuary, a well-known restaurant with mountain views.

Other nearby dining options frequently used by Paradise Valley residents include Il Bracco, Harry & Izzy's, Blanco Cocina + Cantina, and Pomo Pizzeria Napoletana. Because the town keeps commercial density low, most day-to-day retail and additional dining options are a short drive into neighboring Scottsdale or Phoenix.

What should buyers know before purchasing in Paradise Valley?

Paradise Valley has its own municipal zoning and building standards, separate from Phoenix and Scottsdale, including height restrictions and hillside development rules tied to protecting mountain views. Buyers considering new construction or a major renovation should review the town's current requirements early in the process.

Because inventory is limited and lots vary widely in size, usable area, and view corridors, two homes at a similar price point can differ significantly in what they actually offer. Many of the best opportunities in Paradise Valley move through private, off-market channels before they're broadly listed, which is where an agent with local relationships can make the most difference. I spend a lot of time in these subdivisions, and I'm always happy to walk buyers through what's currently available, both on and off market.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about Paradise Valley real estate

What is the average home price in Paradise Valley?

Paradise Valley homes generally range from about $2 million to $30 million or more, depending on lot size, mountain views, and subdivision. Estates in Echo Canyon and Camelback Country Club Estates often sit at the higher end of that range.

Is Paradise Valley part of Phoenix or Scottsdale?

Neither. Paradise Valley is its own incorporated town in Maricopa County, bordered by Phoenix to the west and Scottsdale to the east, with its own zoning and building standards.

Are there gated communities in Paradise Valley?

Some, but gating isn't universal. Much of Paradise Valley is open, non-gated streets. Several subdivisions, including Paradise Valley Country Club Estates and Camelback Country Club Estates, include guard-gated or privately gated sections, and some individual estates add their own perimeter walls and gated drives.

What mountains border Paradise Valley?

Camelback Mountain sits along the town's southern edge and Mummy Mountain rises near its center, and both shape which lots command unobstructed mountain views.

What restaurants are near Paradise Valley?

Elements at Sanctuary and Il Bracco are both near the Sanctuary/El Chorro area, with Harry & Izzy's, Blanco Cocina + Cantina, and Pomo Pizzeria Napoletana also serving the immediate area.

Does Paradise Valley have much commercial development?

No. Paradise Valley intentionally maintains low commercial density compared to neighboring Scottsdale and Phoenix, with most dining and retail concentrated at its edges near resorts such as Sanctuary and El Chorro.
