Community Guide

Central Scottsdale, Arizona real estate

A walkable district anchored by Old Town Scottsdale and Scottsdale Fashion Square, with homes generally ranging from $500,000 to $4 million or more.

What is Central Scottsdale known for?

Central Scottsdale is known for its walkable core around Old Town Scottsdale, the retail anchor of Scottsdale Fashion Square, and a location that sits closer to the geographic center of the Valley than Scottsdale's outlying communities. Homes here range from condominiums and lofts near the entertainment district to sprawling estates in golf and equestrian subdivisions just a few miles away.

The area blends dense, walkable blocks with quieter residential pockets, so a buyer can choose between a residence steps from restaurants and galleries or a gated lot with more separation from the nightlife. That range of housing stock, paired with the central location, is part of what keeps demand steady across price points.

Unlike Scottsdale's northern and outlying communities, where large-lot privacy is the primary draw, Central Scottsdale's identity is built around proximity: to shopping at Fashion Square, to the galleries and restaurants of Old Town, and to the rest of the Valley itself. Architecture styles vary block by block as a result, from mid-century and contemporary condominium buildings near the entertainment district to Southwest and Mediterranean-influenced estates in the area's golf and lake communities.

What do homes cost in Central Scottsdale?

Homes in Central Scottsdale generally price from about $500,000 to $4 million or more, with the spread driven by proximity to Old Town, lot size, and whether a property sits within a guard-gated subdivision. Condos and townhomes near the entertainment district and Fashion Square tend to anchor the lower half of that range, while larger single-family estates in McCormick Ranch and Gainey Ranch typically command the upper half. This year's highest sale in Central Scottsdale was about $7.4 million, above the typical range.

Buyers should confirm current comparable sales for a specific subdivision or property type with a licensed agent, since Central Scottsdale covers a wide mix of housing stock and pricing can shift block by block.

Because the price range spans everything from a one-bedroom condo to a golf-course estate, it is more useful to think of Central Scottsdale as several sub-markets than as a single price band. A loft within walking distance of Old Town's restaurants will price differently than a comparably sized home set back on a quiet interior street in McCormick Ranch, even when the two sit less than a mile apart.

What are Central Scottsdale's notable subdivisions and neighborhoods?

Central Scottsdale includes several well-known subdivisions and districts, each with a distinct character. McCormick Ranch is a large, lake-and-golf-course community with a mix of single-family homes and townhomes built around several small lakes and two golf courses. Gainey Ranch is a resort-style, gated community anchored by the Hyatt Regency Scottsdale Resort and multiple private golf courses, known for its landscaped grounds and gated single-family and condominium sections.

Old Town Scottsdale itself covers the walkable historic and entertainment core, with condos, lofts, and townhomes mixed among galleries, restaurants, and nightlife. The Cactus Corridor, along Cactus Road, includes pockets of larger residential lots with equestrian zoning, giving buyers access to horse property within a short drive of Old Town and Fashion Square.

Each of these areas draws a different kind of buyer's attention for a different reason: McCormick Ranch for its lakes and golf frontage, Gainey Ranch for its resort setting and gated privacy, Old Town for walkability, and the Cactus Corridor for larger lots and horse property. Comparing listings across these subdivisions side by side, rather than treating Central Scottsdale as one uniform market, is usually the fastest way to narrow a search.

What are the lifestyle anchors in Central Scottsdale?

Old Town Scottsdale and Scottsdale Fashion Square anchor daily life in Central Scottsdale, offering walkable access to restaurants, galleries, and retail without leaving the immediate area. The Scottsdale Arts District, centered along Main Street and Marshall Way, carries a long-running ArtWalk tradition on a recurring evening circuit through its galleries. exact ArtWalk schedule

Restaurants frequently mentioned in the area include The Montauk, Toca Madera, Pinyon for coastal Mediterranean fare, Uchi, and Moxies, all within a short drive of Old Town. The Cactus Corridor adds an equestrian dimension to the area, with horse property and riding access available closer to central Scottsdale than in most other parts of the Valley. Because Central Scottsdale sits near the geographic middle of the metro area, it also offers shorter drive times to Phoenix, Tempe, and Scottsdale's northern communities than more outlying subdivisions.

That mix of anchors means a Central Scottsdale property can serve very different day-to-day routines depending on where it sits. A residence within walking distance of Old Town puts galleries, restaurants, and Fashion Square retail on foot, while a property in the Cactus Corridor or the quieter sections of McCormick Ranch trades some of that walkability for larger lots, more separation from nightlife, and, in the Cactus Corridor's case, room for horses.

What should buyers know before purchasing in Central Scottsdale?

Buyers should know that Central Scottsdale covers a wide range of property types, from condos near the entertainment district to gated golf-course estates and equestrian lots, so it helps to narrow a search by subdivision rather than by the area as a whole. Noise levels, walkability, and lot size vary significantly between a residence near Old Town's nightlife corridor and a home in McCormick Ranch or Gainey Ranch.

Because the area includes several HOA-governed communities, including Gainey Ranch and portions of McCormick Ranch, buyers should review each subdivision's HOA rules, fees, and any gate or architectural guidelines directly with the association before writing an offer. An agent familiar with Central Scottsdale's subdivision boundaries can help match a buyer's priorities, whether that's walkability, golf access, or equestrian zoning, to the right pocket of the market.

A property in the Cactus Corridor with equestrian uses also carries its own set of considerations around lot use and setbacks that can differ from a standard residential parcel, so buyers interested in horse property should confirm exactly what a given lot allows before assuming it matches a comparable listing nearby. Given how tightly the area's sub-markets are packed together geographically, working with an agent who tracks Central Scottsdale specifically, rather than Scottsdale broadly, is often the difference between finding the right subdivision quickly and missing it.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about Central Scottsdale real estate

What is the average home price in Central Scottsdale?

Central Scottsdale homes generally range from about $500,000 to $4 million or more, depending on proximity to Old Town, lot size, and subdivision. Estates in McCormick Ranch and Gainey Ranch often sit at the higher end of that range, and this year's highest sale in the area was about $7.4 million.

What subdivisions are part of Central Scottsdale?

Central Scottsdale includes McCormick Ranch, a lake-and-golf-course community; Gainey Ranch, a resort-style gated community anchored by the Hyatt Regency Scottsdale Resort; the walkable core of Old Town Scottsdale; and the Cactus Corridor, known for pockets of equestrian-zoned lots.

Is Old Town Scottsdale walkable?

Yes. Old Town Scottsdale's core is walkable, with condos, lofts, and townhomes mixed among restaurants, galleries, and nightlife within a short walk of Scottsdale Fashion Square.

Are there equestrian properties in Central Scottsdale?

Yes. The Cactus Corridor, along Cactus Road, includes pockets of larger residential lots with equestrian zoning, giving buyers access to horse property closer to central Scottsdale than in most other parts of the Valley.

What restaurants are near Central Scottsdale?

The Montauk, Toca Madera, Pinyon (coastal Mediterranean), Uchi, and Moxies are all restaurants frequently mentioned in the Central Scottsdale area.

How central is Central Scottsdale to the rest of the Valley?
